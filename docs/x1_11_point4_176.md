# The p=5 mod 8 X1(11) point-four divisor

For an accepted X1(11) sample, the Tate point supplies rational order 11 and
the full-`E[2]` gate supplies the previously used group-order divisor 44.
There is a stronger exact statement on the production point-four branch:

> If `p = 5 (mod 8)`, the accepted sample has a retained rational Weber-f
> coordinate and explicit Montgomery image, and the selected Tate twist class
> passes the point-four predicate, then 176 divides its rational group order.

This changes only the known group-order divisor.  The conservative cyclic
divisor remains 44; the implementation does not claim an exact-order-176
point.

## Proof

Put `h=f^6/2`.  The validated Weber/Montgomery identity gives

```text
A^2 - 4 = -h^4.
```

Write the nonzero Montgomery 2-torsion roots as `r,r^-1`.  Since
`p=5 (mod 8)`, a square root `i` of `-1` is a nonsquare, and therefore

```text
r - r^-1 = +/- i h^2
```

is a nonsquare.  The two roots have the same quadratic character.

If `r` is nonsquare, all three root differences are nonsquares.  Passing to
the quadratic twist multiplies their descent characters by a nonsquare, so
all become squares and the twist has full rational `E[4]`, a subgroup of
order 16.

If `r` is square, `(0,0)` has halves at Montgomery x-coordinates `+1` and
`-1`.  Their relevant descent characters have product

```text
chi(1-r) chi(-1-r) = chi(r(r-r^-1)) = -1.
```

Exactly one order-four half is itself divisible by two, giving a rational
point of order eight.  Together with the independent rational 2-torsion this
again gives a subgroup of order 16.  Thus the point-four twist class selected
by the existing predicate is 16-divisible in either structural branch.
Combining this 2-primary subgroup with the independent order-11 point gives a
subgroup of order `16*11=176`.

The retained Weber and explicit-Montgomery hypotheses matter.  A sweep of all
raw X1 equation points found point-four Tate classes of order 88 without that
admission boundary; for example `p=101,x=21,y=61,j=54`.  That j-invariant has
no rational Weber-f lift over `F_101`, so the production generator rejects it.

## Implementation and tests

`group_divisor` is promoted from 88 to 176 exactly when the already validated
`point_four` flag is true and `p mod 8` is 5.  No additional Legendre symbol,
root extraction, or point operation is added.  Other cases remain 44 or 88,
so the p130 target (`p130 mod 8 = 1`) keeps the general point-four divisor 88.

The two pinned `p=157` fixtures exercise both proof branches:

| Index | Tate `(a,b)` | Montgomery `A` | Structure | Exact order |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 7 | `(125,38)` | 15 | rational order-eight branch | 176 |
| 2 | `(70,72)` | 30 | full-`E[4]` twist branch | 176 |

An independent exhaustive sweep covered every eligible prime through 1009.
For the 42 primes `p=5 (mod 8)`, it inspected 18,866 X1 points and 550
Weber-admitted point-four cases: 240 selected the canonical curve, 310 the
twist, and both structural branches occurred.  Every selected order was
divisible by 176.  The congruence restriction is necessary: over
`p=89=1 (mod 8)`, admitted `x=8,y=86,j=29,f=3` has selected order 88, and 390
of 525 admitted `p=1 (mod 8)` cases were not divisible by 176.

The retained exact p125 traces at indices 12--21 all satisfy the new signed
residues: `82,82,82,94,94,82,94,94,94,94` modulo 176.  Exact orders at
indices 17, 19, and 20 are 176 modulo 352, establishing that another universal
factor of two would be false.

The semantic policy is schedule-bound as

```text
weber-full-e2-mod4-if-validated-x1-selected-group-divisor-point4-p5mod8-176-v2
```

and the generator metadata is version 2.  A checkpoint created before this
strengthening cannot be resumed as the new identity.
